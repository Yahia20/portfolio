Stop losing money to hotel cancellations

I analysed 119,000 hotel bookings to find out what cancellations cost, who cancels, and what the hotel should do about it.

The problem

More than a third of hotel bookings are cancelled. While a booking is active, the hotel can't sell that room to anyone else, so a cancellation can leave it empty. At the two hotels in this data, cancelled bookings were worth €16.7M over 26 months. Asking every guest for a deposit looks like the fix, but many guests then book somewhere else. The hotel needs to know which guests to ask.

How it works

Step 1 / 5

Check the data

28 automatic checks confirm that no booking is lost and no euro is counted twice. Clear errors, like a €5,400 night, are removed and listed.

Step 2 / 5

Find who cancels

Bookings made a year ahead cancel 68% of the time; bookings made in the last week, 10%. Guests who cancelled before cancel again 92% of the time.

Step 3 / 5

Score every booking

A model gives each new booking a risk score from 0% to 100%. It uses only what the hotel knows at the moment of booking.

Step 4 / 5

Test the rule

Rule: if the risk score is 26% or higher, ask for the first night in advance. The rule was tested on 2017 bookings under 12 different scenarios.

Step 5 / 5

Show the answer

A dashboard in English and Arabic. Every number comes from the data, and every chart has a table behind it.

Results

Tested on Jan–Aug 2017 bookings · October 2026

  • Asking only the riskiest 45% of bookings for the first night would have earned €399k in eight months. Asking every guest would have lost €199k.
  • The rule makes money in all 12 scenarios tested, from €147k to €1.2M.
  • The model predicts the number of cancellations each week with a 9% average error. Hotels use this number to decide how many extra rooms they can safely sell.
  • The model is right 78 times out of 100 on bookings it never saw. Many published versions of this analysis claim 95–99% because they use information that only exists after the guest arrives. This project measures that gap.

How this can help your business

Ask the right guests

A deposit only where the risk is high, so you keep the guests who were going to come.

Plan overbooking on a number

Expected cancellations per week, per hotel, from the bookings you already have.

Numbers you can defend

Every number is checked automatically, so finance and operations see the same figures.

Hotels, travel agencies, clinics, event venues and car rental — any business that holds a place for a customer who might not come.

Good to know

  • The data is public — two hotels in Portugal, 2015–2017, in euros. The method works for any similar business; the numbers come from each business's own data.
  • How many guests refuse a deposit is an assumption, because the data can't show it. That is why results cover 12 scenarios. Before a full rollout, try the rule on a share of bookings first.

  • dbt warehouse on DuckDB (staging, facts, marts) with 28 data tests, including reconciliation tests: every source row is kept or excluded by a named rule, and revenue agrees to the cent by booking, night and month.
  • Leakage audit: columns filled in after booking (parking, assigned room, booking changes, corrected country) are excluded. The score inflation from leaky columns and random splits is measured and published (77.6% honest vs 88.7%).
  • Monthly walk-forward scoring: each 2017 month is scored by a LightGBM model retrained on all earlier arrivals. ROC AUC 0.850, Brier 0.150, calibration checked per risk band.
  • Policy backtest: threshold chosen on Sep–Dec 2016, applied unchanged to 2017, with a sensitivity grid over walk-away and resale rates. One command rebuilds everything in about two minutes on a laptop.
