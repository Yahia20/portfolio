The problem

Most customer questions to a travel agency have a written answer: how much to change a Saver ticket, when a refund arrives, how many bags a child gets. The team answers them by hand, again and again, in WhatsApp and on the phone. A chatbot looks like the fix, but a chatbot that makes up a refund deadline or promises compensation costs more than it saves. The real requirement is an assistant that answers only what the policy says, in the way Saudi customers actually write, and hands everything else to a person.

How it works

Step 1 / 4

Find the rule

Each message is matched to the closest sections of the company policy, in Arabic or English.

Step 2 / 4

Decide

The AI chooses: answer from the policy, hand over to a person, or politely redirect an off-topic message.

Step 3 / 4

Check with code

An answer is sent only if every fee and deadline in it is written in the policy. Otherwise a person gets it.

Step 4 / 4

Hand over cleanly

Complaints, compensation, a specific booking, emergencies and anything not in the policy go to a person with a one-line summary.

Results

245 test messages written before the code · October 2026

  • Every one of the 163 answers it sent was right — the right rule and every figure the question needed.
  • All 68 messages that needed a person reached one — complaints, compensation, actions on a booking, emergencies and legal threats.
  • By language: Saudi dialect 124 of 126, formal Arabic 40 of 40, English 79 of 79.
  • The 2 policy questions it did not answer itself were safe misses — they went to a person instead of getting a wrong reply.
  • The same test on a model that costs 5 times more: it sent 4 wrong answers and missed 3 hand-overs, so the cheaper model is the one that ships.

How this can help your business

Routine questions answered

Fees, refunds, baggage, visas and payment, in the customer's own dialect, any time of day.

No invented promises

Code blocks any fee or deadline that isn't in your policy before a customer sees it.

Your team gets the hard cases

Complaints and exceptions arrive with a summary, so the agent starts with the context.

Travel agencies, hotels, clinics, telecom and e-commerce — any business with written policies and customers who ask the same questions every day.

Good to know

  • The company and its policy are fictional, written for this project. For a real business, the assistant reads your own policy.
  • The test uses single messages, not long conversations.
  • The marking is strict: one of the expensive model's 4 wrong answers was a correct reply that wrote "two bags" in words instead of the number.
  • If you want it to give emergency phone numbers, they have to be written into the policy; otherwise it hands the message to a person.

Technical detailsFor engineers and recruiters
  • Python
  • LLMs
  • RAG
  • LLM evaluation
  • sentence-transformers
  • DeepSeek
  • OpenAI SDK
  • Pydantic
  • Retrieval: every policy line embedded in Arabic and English (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2); a section scores as its best line plus a word-overlap bonus; top 5 sections plus an index of all 14, with one optional `need_section` lookup. The right section reached the model for 165 of 165 policy questions.
  • One JSON call per message (action, reply, cited sections, hand-over reason and agent summary). A code check sends an answer only if it cites a shown section and every number in it is in that section or one arithmetic step from the customer's numbers; hand-over replies get the same number check.
  • Frozen test (245 messages: 126 Saudi dialect, 40 formal Arabic, 79 English) committed before the assistant code; tuning on a separate 78-message dev set; marking by code only; responses cached and spend capped.
